# Workshop, Singularity City, and Singularity Software Inc.

This document records the intended relationship between the public Workshop, the persistent Singularity City, and the future code-creation technology of Singularity Software Inc.

## The Workshop is the tooling playground

The Singularity Workshop exists to **present tooling and let people play with it**.

The Workshop is not a static product catalog and it is not merely a marketing wrapper around the software. Its rooms and Experiences are executable demonstrations of the technologies that make the ecosystem possible.

The core loop is:

```text
ENTER
  |
  v
EXPERIENCE
  |
  v
PLAY WITH THE TOOL
  |
  v
CHANGE SOMETHING
  |
  v
OBSERVE THE CONSEQUENCE
  |
  v
UNDERSTAND
  |
  v
CREATE
```

Examples include:

- **Forge** — build and manipulate FSM-oriented compositions.
- **Laboratory** — experiment with physics and computation through physical-looking experiments rather than forms.
- **Rendering research** — expose observer-relative representation, Event Horizons, and eventually the hidden 2D/3D continuum.
- **Spatial Workshop** — explore a world in which capabilities and objects are things the visitor can encounter and manipulate.
- **Other Workshop instruments** — present additional capabilities as places, tools, experiments, or Experiences rather than implementation-name pages.

The visitor should be able to learn by doing.

## Singularity Software Inc. is separate

**Singularity Software Inc. is its own beast.**

It represents the future code-creation technology: visualized, agent-backed coding and software construction. It should not be confused with the Workshop's role as a technology playground.

The Workshop may eventually provide a **helipad** for Singularity Software Inc. When that software is functional enough to expose publicly, the helipad becomes a physical transition from the Workshop to the rooftop helipad of the Software Inc. building.

Until then, the Workshop should not pretend that the Software Inc. product already exists as a finished destination.

```text
WORKSHOP
   |
   +-- tooling / experiments / creation
   |
   +-- HELIPAD
          |
          | when Software Inc. is functional
          v
SINGULARITY SOFTWARE INC.
   |
   v
ROOFTOP HELIPAD
```

## Singularity City is persistent background proof

Singularity City is not the primary product destination of the Workshop.

It is **background noise in the best possible sense**: a persistent, increasingly populated demonstration that the architecture can sustain a living digital world.

The City should be populated by **Digitens**. Digitens are persistent digital inhabitants whose behavior is governed by the same composable runtime principles as everything else. They commute, work, travel, visit places, and live lives while the visitor is doing something completely different.

The City therefore keeps proving the system without asking the visitor to watch a demo.

```text
visitor uses Forge
       |
       +---- outside the window ----> Digitens commute
       |
       +---- distant City ----------> buildings persist
       |
       +---- Workshop heart --------> capabilities continue running
```

## Digital real estate

The long-term purpose of Singularity City is digital real estate.

Initially, digital real estate can be free. The important architectural milestone is persistence: a place can exist, be created, changed, occupied, and revisited.

The eventual model is that users can create real-world-looking shops and other businesses inside the persistent City. Those businesses become spatial manifestations of user-created data and capabilities.

The Workshop provides the scaffolding, framework, ecosystem, interoperability rules, and composition machinery. Users should ultimately be able to build within those rules and own the resulting presence.

## The Ontology Mall

One planned City landmark is a massive **Ontology Mall**.

The mall reverses the usual visual metaphor of an ontology:

- the top of the ontology becomes the bottom of the building;
- each ontology layer becomes a floor;
- each layer is divided into parallel departments;
- departments at the same layer are deliberately separate buildings/areas;
- movement between ontology layers occurs through the layer above or below rather than arbitrary lateral access.

Conceptually:

```text
                 ontology top
                     |
              +------+------+
              |    FLOOR    |
              +------+------+
                     |
          +----------+----------+
          |          |          |
       department department department
          |          |          |
          +----------+----------+
                     |
                 next layer
                     |
                    ...
                     |
                 ontology root
```

This is both architecture made spatial and a future commercial environment.

## Commerce belongs inside the Experience

The eventual shopping model is not supposed to be a separate e-commerce page bolted onto the game.

A visitor playing an Experience may encounter a shop created by another user.
That shop can sell digital goods, real-world paired goods, services, or other permitted products. The visitor can purchase what they need **while playing**.

The important relationship is:

```text
USER CREATES SHOP
       |
       v
SINGULARITY CITY
       |
       +---- spatial shop
       |
       +---- ontology manifestation
       |
       +---- Experience manifestation
       |
       +---- real-world storefront
       |
       v
VISITOR DISCOVERS SHOP WHILE PLAYING
       |
       v
PURCHASE
```

The same underlying shop should not need to be rebuilt separately for every Experience. Its semantic identity, ontology position, products, rules, and capabilities should be reusable while each Experience may choose how the shop is manifested.

## Make creation ridiculously easy

When monetization is enabled, the hard part should not be learning a proprietary 3D editor.

A user should be able to create and control a shop from the Workshop's composition vocabulary:

- what the shop sells;
- how it is organized in the Singularity ontology;
- how it appears in Singularity City;
- what it looks like in a particular Experience;
- what capabilities it exposes;
- what real-world products are paired with digital products;
- what presentation/representation is appropriate at different Event Horizons.

The Workshop provides the scaffolding.

The user provides the creation.

The ecosystem provides interoperability.

The eventual transaction system provides commerce.

## The economic direction

The long-term business model is intentionally simple at the architectural level:

> **Build the scaffolding, let users build the world, make participation useful enough that users can earn from what they create, and participate in the transactions enabled by the ecosystem.**

Monetization is not the reason the Workshop exists. It is the eventual consequence of making the sandbox useful enough that people want to build businesses inside it.

No commercial capability should be represented as functional until its implementation and trust boundaries actually exist.

## The Workshop remains the heart

The persistent City can become enormous.

The Ontology Mall can become enormous.

The number of Digitens can become enormous.

The number of shops and Experiences can become enormous.

The Workshop remains the heart because it is where the machinery is presented, understood, and used to create more machinery and worlds.

The City proves persistence.

The Workshop proves creation.

The Laboratory proves physical/computational reasoning.

The Forge proves composition.

The future Software Inc. proves agent-backed software creation.

Together they form a coherent public argument:

> **The Singularity Workshop is the sandbox and scaffolding. The people who enter it are supposed to build the world.**
