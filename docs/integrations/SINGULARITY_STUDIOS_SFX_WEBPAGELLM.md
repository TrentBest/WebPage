# Singularity Studios — WebPageLLM Integration Brief

**Audience:** WebPageLLM and anyone shaping the public Workshop/WebPage experience  
**Status:** product direction and integration contract; not a claim that the studio or acoustic simulation is implemented  
**Source branch:** `development`  
**Do not publish or deploy from this brief.**

## The intent

**Singularity Studios belongs in the WebPage experience as a first-class creative destination.** It should be more than a link to a sound library or a static “SFX” feature card. It is the place in Singularity City where creators make, inspect, compare, and share audiovisual work—and where the Workshop can demonstrate that sound is connected to physical causes instead of being bolted onto events as arbitrary effects.

The experience should join two complementary workflows:

1. **Creative studio:** creators can audition and organize reference sounds, build scenes, compare takes, and eventually produce or share authored audio/visual works.
2. **Physical sound lab:** visitors can follow a physical event from source to propagation to listener, then compare how the same source is heard in different spaces and from different listener positions.

Keep these workflows visibly related but conceptually distinct. A creator may author a soundtrack or an intentional cinematic effect. A simulated sword impact, metal panel, engine, electrical arc, breaking object, or footstep should not need a hand-authored “play this SFX now” instruction to become audible. The physical model should produce a justified acoustic source; the acoustic environment and listener then determine the observation.

## Film production and reusable sets

Singularity Studios is also the Workshop's own film-production campus: enormous indoor stages, reusable sets and props, camera placements, camera/lighting rigs, shot plans, choreography, and takes. This production workflow is specified in [`SINGULARITY_STUDIOS_PRODUCTION_MODEL.md`](../SINGULARITY_STUDIOS_PRODUCTION_MODEL.md). Sound & Acoustics is one capability inside that broader studio—not the definition of the entire studio.

Keep the production model data-first: canonical assets are referenced by stable identity/version, while placements, overrides, camera paths, cues, and takes are separate records. Multiple camera views must observe the same scene entities rather than cloning the world. A large indoor battle stage is a scale target to measure, not a claim of current renderer capacity.

## Where it belongs

- Add **Singularity Studios** as a first-class destination in the WebPage / Singularity City discovery journey, alongside Labs, schools, arenas, dojos, transit, and other city experiences.
- Inside Studios, make **Sound & Acoustics** a clear sub-experience. It should be reachable from the studio itself and from the Physics/acoustics learning material.
- Preserve the single WebPage host and existing manifest/hub conventions. Do not introduce a competing router, second application heartbeat, or a second implementation of reusable physics.
- Treat the studio as a place/experience that can eventually host creator-owned, customizable rooms and tools. Do not invent a complete creator economy, publishing pipeline, or real-time mixer before those capabilities exist.

## First useful visitor journey

1. Enter Singularity Studios.
2. Choose a sound source with honest provenance: a licensed/reference recording, a captured recording, or a sound generated by an explicitly identified model.
3. Place the source and listener in a simple scene.
4. Compare the same source in a free-field baseline and in spaces such as a small furnished room, a large hall, or behind a barrier/doorway.
5. Move the listener and compare what changes.
6. Inspect the accompanying SVG: source, space, listener, distances, timing, and the measurements or assumptions behind each comparison.
7. Hear the source through normal HTML audio controls, with direct-file fallback. The SVG communicates the scene; it should not be relied upon as a cross-browser audio player.

This can start as a bounded, evidence-labelled demonstration. Do not gate all useful presentation on SVG click interception: use ordinary HTML controls and accessible buttons around the SVG, and make the visualization informative even when the SVG itself is not interactive.

## Current evidence boundary

The Physics repository currently provides an analytical free-field observation primitive: source/listener positions, separation, propagation delay at a fixed speed of sound, direction, and estimated distance-based dB SPL. It does **not** yet generate a pressure waveform or audible output, nor does it model room reflections, reverberation, occlusion, diffraction, material-driven acoustic excitation, or binaural perception.

A browser-playable reference recording is useful for demonstrating the audio presentation path, but it is **not Physics-generated sound**. A room comparison is not a real simulation until the room model produces the displayed result from a documented algorithm and parameters. Label each artifact as one of:

- **Reference audio** — an existing recording with known source/license/provenance.
- **Measured result** — recorded from a stated physical setup with capture details.
- **Model output** — generated by a named/versioned model with stated assumptions.
- **Conceptual illustration** — explanatory art, not evidence of a computed result.

Do not mix these labels or let a decorative waveform imply solver output.

## Ownership boundaries

| Concern | Owner |
|---|---|
| Physical contact, motion, materials, deformation, damage, energy accounting, acoustic-source coupling, propagation observations | Physics and the relevant domain packages |
| State-machine behavior and scheduled process execution | FSM_API |
| Capability composition and runtime assembly | FSM_COS |
| Reusable studio/audio domain contracts, once identified and implemented | A focused Workshop package/MicroBundle, not WebPage-only business logic |
| Browser controls, accessible playback, scene presentation, navigation, viewport/input adaptation, evidence labels | WebPage and the appropriate GUI presentation layer |
| Reference recordings and creator assets | Explicit asset/source/license contract; never silently imply they came from Physics |

WebPage should consume authoritative Physics observations and generated artifacts rather than reimplementing acoustic calculations. The visual must derive from the same scenario/evidence record as the numbers it presents.

## The physical-causality rule

The target chain is:

`physical event → material/structure response → acoustic excitation → propagation through space → listener observation`

For example, a metal impact should not directly trigger a generic “metal hit” clip as a simulation shortcut. Contact dynamics can establish impulse and contact conditions; a declared material/source-coupling model must then explain how those conditions produce an acoustic signal. A room model transforms that signal, and the listener observes the result. Until those contracts exist, use licensed reference audio as reference audio and clearly mark the gap.

This does not prohibit creative sound design. It separates **authored creative audio** from **sound claimed to be caused by the simulated world**.

## Delivery slices

### Slice 1 — Studio front door and audio provenance
- Establish a visible Singularity Studios destination in the current WebPage hub/navigation pattern.
- Add a Sound & Acoustics experience with a reference recording and ordinary HTML playback controls.
- Show provenance, license, and an explicit “reference audio—not Physics output” label.
- Include an SVG scene/causality view and direct audio link fallback.
- Verify keyboard access, narrow viewport behavior, and that the host remains the single navigation/runtime boundary.

### Slice 2 — Evidence-backed free-field comparison
- Drive the display from actual Physics `FreeFieldAcoustics.Observe` observations and a versioned scenario record.
- Compare source/listener distance, direction, propagation delay, and estimated level without inventing waveform data.
- Generate the SVG from the same record; verify the renderer output in CI.

### Slice 3 — One honest room-response model
- Agree on a minimal, explicit room-response contract and assumptions before implementing the UI.
- Add a small number of controlled spaces, then compare the same input source under those conditions.
- Generate audible model output only after the signal representation, sample rate, channel/amplitude conventions, deterministic behavior, and transformation are specified and tested.
- Keep the free-field baseline available so the room effect can be isolated.

### Slice 4 — Material-driven sound from physical events
- Couple contact/fracture/deformation/electrical or other source events to acoustics through declared models.
- Track energy and provenance; do not label unexplained energy loss as sound, heat, or damage.
- Extend the same source through propagation and listener observation; add material-specific examples only when the model supports them.

## Acceptance bar

- Singularity Studios is discoverable from the main WebPage journey without a competing host/router.
- Creative/reference audio is clearly distinguished from measured and Physics-generated output.
- The SVG and numeric evidence come from the same scenario or observation record.
- Room comparisons are based on an implemented model, not hand-adjusted illustration.
- Playback uses browser-native HTML audio controls with a direct-link fallback; SVG interaction is optional.
- Physics remains the authority for physical observations; WebPage does not duplicate its formulas.
- Current, planned, and unavailable capabilities are visibly distinguished.
- Build, tests, browser smoke checks, and any evidence-generation checks pass before claiming the slice complete.

**North star:** Singularity Studios should let people create sound, hear sound, and understand why a world sounds the way it does—without confusing creative authorship with physical causality.
