# Tabletop Content Boundary

## Purpose

The Workshop can provide a **table**, not somebody else's game.

A tabletop Experience gives a Game Master spatial and presentation utilities while
leaving actual rules, setting, characters, names, artwork, maps, and other
creator-supplied content to the creator.

## What the Workshop provides

- shared tabletop surface;
- Game Master control surface;
- tile and terrain placement;
- piece/token/miniature placement;
- per-seat views;
- player avatars;
- visibility/fog controls;
- annotations and measurements;
- snapshots;
- optional neutral turn-order presentation;
- creator-defined controls and instructions.

The engine does not need to know which published or homebrew game is being played.

## Intellectual-property guardrail

Do not implement a simplistic rule such as "change something substantial and it
becomes legal." Copyright, trademark, trade dress, publicity, contract,
licensing, and other doctrines can apply differently to different material and
jurisdictions.

The eventual **Content Boundary / Transformation Advisor** should:

1. identify what a creator is supplying;
2. record ownership and claimed rights;
3. distinguish creator-owned material from platform primitives;
4. flag names, logos, artwork, text, characters, settings, and other potentially
   protected material for manual review;
5. suggest neutral transformations where appropriate;
6. preserve original and transformed provenance separately;
7. require creator confirmation rather than declaring something "legal";
8. provide a takedown/contact path and an auditable rights history.

The advisor is a workflow aid, not a legal opinion.

## Commercial licensing

A future rights marketplace can let an IP owner voluntarily publish or license
content into the Workshop. Commercial terms should be negotiated explicitly.
The platform should not assume that a litigation threat creates permission to use
somebody else's property.

## Architectural consequence

Tabletop is another MicroBundle family:

Tabletop utilities -> creator content -> arbitration -> Experience -> spatial table

The same primitives can serve RPG campaigns, board-game-like Experiences,
tactical simulations, education, collaborative planning, miniature/stagecraft
environments, and other creator-authored Experiences.

The environment remains neutral. The content defines the game.
